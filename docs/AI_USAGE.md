## Where the AI was wrong and we caught it

* v1.0 refused the course's `Ex0.csv` entirely ("no planes found") — a format guess that was
  never tested against the real file. Lesson: test against the teacher's artefacts first.
* v1.0's fail-fast policy would have produced no video for a file with one bad row; item 7c
  asks for a frame per row.
* v2.x treated the window app as an "extra" and the video command as the product; the exercise
  is the other way round (the app that cuts and shows the model, then extracts the video for the
  use cases). We said so; the AI rebuilt the front end and the documents around the app.
* The app's first CSV writer rounded to 3 decimals, which broke the `EPS = 0.001` rule for
  oblique planes on the way back in and made the reader choose a crossed pairing of the points.
  Caught by rendering the exported CSV with the demo and comparing the frames.
* v3.0 read the four points of a trapezoid in the Q&A order `(a1, a2, b, c)` and only fell back
  to "rails" when that was invalid. The lecturer's own files list them rail by rail
  (`a1, b, a2, c`); in the Q&A order every rectangle of his use cases would have come out
  transposed. Caught only once the AI read his test data — lesson (again): his artefacts first.
* v3.0's 3-D view showed three slices in a box: correct, but not what he asked for — the object
  itself, cut open, with its inside visible. We pointed at his words and his Figure 1.
* The CSV writer's 6 decimals still changed one frame of the lecturer's `head.csv` on the way
  through the app; exact numbers (shortest round-trip decimal) fixed it for good.
* A unit test expected `grey(150) == 128` for the window `[100, 200]`; 127.5 is a rounding tie
  that floating point resolves to 127. The test, not the code, was wrong.
* v3.1's Export refused to run until planes were collected by hand, and Play was disabled
  without them: the app made the user do the work the button promised. The video is now
  whatever Play shows, so Export always has something to save.
* v3.2 trusted any program called `ffmpeg` it could start. A copy without the H.264 encoder (or
  one that fails) turned every export into an error; nothing in our tests had such a copy. Now
  ffmpeg is asked first and the built-in encoder takes over when it fails — tested with fakes.
* The "4 points" button had never been clicked in a test: the robot scripts reached 4 points
  only through the presets. Clicking it redrew the old triangle, which set the button back.
* `--strip-debug` looked harmless; on a Linux host it rewrote the Windows launchers and silently
  broke their signatures. Nothing on Linux or Wine checks Authenticode, so only a real Windows PC
  showed it. Lesson: verify what you ship on the platform it ships to, signatures included.
* The FPS in the toolbar was a correct measurement shown at the wrong time: a still plane's
  projection speed, standing next to a video that played at 10 fps. Correct numbers still need
  to mean something to the person reading them. And "fast" was tested on the algorithm only: the
  3-D view on the Swing thread made the whole window wait, which no unit test measures — Robot
  timings of Play while dragging do now.
